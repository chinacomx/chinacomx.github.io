> **Where Instruction Meets Graphic Narratives: Environmental Storytelling and Visual Rhetorics in Chinese Children’s Comics**
>
> Comics have long been used for environmental education. This article examines how Chinese environmental comics for younger readers integrate educational content into visual forms. Using Shalom M. Fisch’s distinction between educational content and narrative content as a point of departure, I analyze three groups of texts: early socialist comics with political eco-motifs, post-1980s popular science comics, and recent story-based comics shaped by intertextual adaptation. These works develop many strategies to integrate their stories with the information they wish to convey. Early comics build environmental norms into model narratives of exemplary children. Information comics employ guide figures and ironic humor to organize and communicate knowledge. More recent story comics draw on cultural texts, from classical literature to popular media, and the intertextuality supports the integration of environmental instruction within existing narrative frameworks. The cases discussed restructure how knowledge is presented and render environmental norms repeatable. The study thus contributes to broader debates in environmental children’s literature by highlighting approaches to environmental instruction and storytelling. Here, guidance and narrative integration play a central role.
